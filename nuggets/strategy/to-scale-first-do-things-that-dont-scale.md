# To Scale, First Do Things That Don't Scale

_Last updated: 2025-12-16_

Paul Graham's influential essay challenges the myth that startups either magically "take off" or don't. Instead, successful founders actively make their startups succeed by doing unscalable things in the early days. This counterintuitive advice has become foundational wisdom at Y Combinator.

The core insight: manually recruit users, obsess over making early customers deliriously happy, and focus narrowly before expanding. Facebook started only at Harvard, achieving critical mass there before spreading to other schools. Stripe's founders literally visited users to install their software in person.

Why it works:
- Creates an "insanely great" experience that generates word-of-mouth
- Provides deep customer insights you can't get from analytics
- Builds a foundation of passionate early advocates
- Gives you feedback loops to iterate rapidly

For product managers:
- Conduct 1-on-1 onboarding calls with first 100 users
- Personally respond to every support ticket or feature request
- Build custom solutions for early customers to learn their needs
- Choose a tiny, focused market before expanding horizontally
