# Three Types of Negotiators

_Last updated: 2025-12-16_

In "Never Split the Difference," former FBI hostage negotiator Chris Voss identifies three fundamental negotiation styles: Analyst, Accommodator, and Assertive. Understanding these types—both in yourself and others—helps product managers navigate stakeholder negotiations, vendor discussions, and cross-functional alignment.

The three types:

**Analyst**: Methodical and data-driven. Views negotiation as intellectual problem-solving. Focuses intensely on understanding before being understood. Values thorough preparation and logical arguments. Can appear cold or slow to decide.

**Accommodator**: Relationship-first approach. Values building trust and rapport above immediate wins. Time spent together equals relationship strength. Optimistic and sociable, but may avoid conflict. Silence feels like tension.

**Assertive**: Direct and results-oriented. Being heard is the priority. Time equals money; silence is an opportunity to speak more. Loves winning and moving fast. Risk of tunnel vision on their own goals.

For product managers:
- Identify your default style to understand your blind spots
- Adapt approach based on stakeholder's negotiation type
- With analysts: Bring data and give time to process
- With accommodators: Build relationship before pushing hard
- With assertives: Be direct and respect their time
