# Information Radiator

_Last updated: 2025-12-16_

An information radiator is a highly visible display that shows a team's current progress and key project information at a glance. The term was coined by Alistair Cockburn in 2001 to describe displays placed in high-traffic areas where team members and stakeholders can easily see updates.

Unlike static reports, information radiators are dynamic and frequently updated as teams move through different stages of development. They promote transparency and accountability by making work visible to everyone.

Common examples include Kanban boards, burndown charts, velocity charts, and sprint boards. The key is that anyone passing by can understand the team's status without asking questions.

**Key characteristics:**

- Placed in highly visible, easily accessible locations
- Updated frequently to reflect current project state
- Simple enough to understand at a glance
- Promotes responsibility and transparency across the team
- Can be physical (whiteboards) or digital displays
