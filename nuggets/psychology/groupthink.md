# Groupthink

_Last updated: 2025-12-16_

Groupthink is the psychological phenomenon where the desire for harmony and consensus in a group results in irrational or dysfunctional decision-making. Members suppress dissenting viewpoints, fail to critically analyze alternatives, and isolate themselves from outside influences.

Yale psychologist Irving Janis coined the term in 1972 after studying major policy disasters including the Bay of Pigs invasion, Pearl Harbor, and Vietnam War escalation. He identified how cohesive groups systematically make poor decisions when consensus becomes more important than critical evaluation.

**Warning signs of groupthink:**
- Illusion of invulnerability leading to excessive risk-taking
- Collective rationalization dismissing warnings or negative feedback
- Unquestioned belief in the group's inherent morality
- Stereotyping those who oppose the group as weak or uninformed
- Self-censorship where doubts are suppressed to maintain harmony
- Illusion of unanimity where silence is interpreted as agreement

**For product teams:**
- Shipping features everyone privately doubts will succeed
- Ignoring user research that contradicts the team's vision
- Dismissing competitive threats or market feedback
