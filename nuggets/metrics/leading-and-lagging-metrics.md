# Leading and Lagging Metrics

_Last updated: 2025-12-16_

Leading indicators predict future outcomes and measure activities that drive results. Lagging indicators measure outcomes that have already occurred. You need both: leading metrics tell you what to do, lagging metrics tell you if it worked.

Think of leading metrics as inputs (controllable actions) and lagging metrics as outputs (results). Leading metrics are predictive and influenceable; lagging metrics are historical and confirmatory.

**Examples for product teams:**

Leading metrics (predictive):
- Number of user research interviews conducted
- Feature adoption rate in first 30 days
- Support ticket response time
- Weekly active users (predicts monthly retention)
- Trial-to-paid conversion rate

Lagging metrics (outcome):
- Monthly recurring revenue
- Annual churn rate
- Net Promoter Score
- Market share
- Customer lifetime value

**Why both matter:**
- Lagging metrics show if you're winning but don't tell you how to improve
- Leading metrics are actionable but don't guarantee business success
- You need leading metrics to course-correct before lagging metrics show failure
- Product strategy should connect leading metrics to lagging business outcomes

**Best practice:** Work backward from lagging indicators to identify which leading indicators influence them, then focus your team on moving those leading metrics.
