# Captive Product Pricing

_Last updated: 2025-12-14_

**Captive product pricing** sells a core product at low price (or loss) to drive demand for complementary products that generate recurring revenue. "Captive" products are necessary to use the main product, creating ongoing revenue and lock-in. This strategy requires balancing customer lifetime value, competitive dynamics, and risks from third-party alternatives eroding captive product margins. Apple set core devices with premium pricing (value-based) but making high-margin profits on necessary add-ons, ensuring customers feel "captivated" by value, not trapped by forced purchases.

Effective when:
- Core product requires specific/proprietary consumables/accessories
- High switching costs after initial investment, ecosystem lock-in (software, services)
- Complementary products have higher margins
- Potential for recurring revenue over lifecycle

**Examples**:
- Razors and blades (Gillette)
- Printers and ink (HP, Canon, Epson)
- Gaming consoles and games (PlayStation, Xbox)
- Coffee machines and pods (Nespresso)
