# Yak Shaving

_Last updated: 2025-12-16_

Yak shaving is when you get sidetracked by a seemingly endless chain of small, tangential tasks that bear no obvious relationship to your original goal. The term was coined at MIT's AI Lab in the 1990s by Carlin Vieri, inspired by a Ren & Stimpy episode.

The classic example: You're writing a thesis and need a reference, so you fire up your email client to search for it. But the email client has a bug, so you download the latest version. That requires updating your operating system. Now you're six steps removed from writing your thesis, shaving a metaphorical yak.

**Why PMs should care:**
- Destroys flow state and derails sprint goals
- Reveals scope creep and lack of focus
- Each tangent spawns more tangents, exponentially wasting time
- Teams can spend days on tasks that should be quick wins

**Common yak shaving traps:**
- "Let's just refactor this one thing before adding the feature..."
- "We need to fix the dev environment before we can test the fix..."
- "Before we ship, we should update the entire design system..."
- "Let's reorganize the entire repository structure first..."

**How to avoid it:**
- Timebox tangential tasks and defer non-critical work
- Ask: "Is this blocking my original goal right now?"
- Create a backlog item for the tangent and return to your main task
- Use the "two-hops" rule: if you're more than two steps removed, stop
