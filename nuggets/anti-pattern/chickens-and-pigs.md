# Chickens and Pigs

_Last updated: 2025-12-16_

The "Chickens and Pigs" metaphor comes from a joke about opening a restaurant: the chicken offers eggs (involvement), while the pig offers bacon (commitment). In Scrum's early days, this distinguished between team members who were "committed" (developers, Scrum Master, Product Owner) versus "involved" (stakeholders, managers).

The metaphor was officially removed from the Scrum Guide in 2011. Why? It created problematic hierarchies that contradicted Scrum's core values of openness and collaboration. Labeling stakeholders as "less committed chickens" alienated important contributors and discouraged inclusive participation.

Why it's now considered an anti-pattern:
- Creates artificial hierarchy between team roles
- Dismisses valuable stakeholder input and involvement
- Contradicts agile principles of collaboration and respect
- Can lead to excluding important voices from the process

What to do instead:
- Recognize all participants contribute valuable perspectives
- Focus on role clarity without value judgments
- Encourage stakeholder engagement rather than gatekeeping
- Use inclusive language that respects different forms of contribution
