# Black Swan Events

_Last updated: 2025-12-14_

**Black Swan Events** (Nassim Taleb) are rare, unpredictable occurrences with extreme impact that seem predictable in hindsight. Three characteristics: Rarity (outside regular expectations), Extreme impact (massive consequences), Retrospective predictability ("we should have seen it coming").

**Examples**:
- **Negative**: 9/11, 2008 financial crisis, COVID-19, Fukushima, dot-com burst
- **Positive**: Internet/WWW rise, personal computers, penicillin, smartphones, Berlin Wall fall

Product management assumes predictable futures (roadmaps, forecasts, plans). Black Swan thinking challenges this—over-optimizing for predictable scenarios leaves you vulnerable to the unpredictable. Implications: Post-launch reviews construct tidy stories, ignoring randomness and luck. Build resilience, redundancy — minimize catastrophic failures. Stay flexible to pivot when opportunities emerge. Tips: Scenario planning with humility, build in buffers, diversify risk, move fast to exploit randomness, question narratives, value flexibility over efficiency.

**Key takeaway**: Focus on consequences, not probabilities. Design for resilience against negatives and positioning for positives. The future is more uncertain than roadmaps suggest.
